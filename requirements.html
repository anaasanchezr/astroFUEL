Mission Requirements Revision
version 2 – 23/10/2026

Before entering into the MAR, it is necessary to present the revision made to the Mission Requirements, due to updates in the mission definition and analysis.

Revision colour convention

Text shown in red reproduces the modifications highlighted in the official Mission Requirements Revision included in the Mission Analysis Report.

BASELINE GEO servicing

Cooperative GEO clients using xenon station-keeping and compatible refuelling interfaces.

SPACECRAFT 4,500 kg limit

Updated maximum wet-mass constraint and new launch dimensional envelope.

OPERATIONS RPO safety

New rendezvous, proximity, communication, hold-point and autonomous-abort requirements.

02 / MISSION LEVEL REQUIREMENTS

Mission requirements.

The following requirements constitute Mission Requirements Revision version 2. Red text identifies the modifications introduced during the mission definition and analysis update.

MIS_010 Client satellite compatibility ⌄

The satellite shall be capable of providing refuelling services for valuable GEO satellites weighing between roughly 1,000 kg and an estimated 6,500 kg at launch, provided that they have a compatible refuelling interface and that they use xenon for their station-keeping.

MIS_020 RAFTI refuelling interface ⌄

The satellite shall have a mechanical interface compatible with the OrbitFab standard refueling port RAFTI , allowing a safe and stable connection during xenon transfer, as well as a controlled and safe undocking once the operation is completed.

MIS_040 Operational lifetime and disposal ⌄

The satellite shall have a minimum operational lifetime of 8 years after insertion into its nominal orbit, during which it shall maintain the capabilities required to perform the planned refuelling services. After performing all of them and once the payload xenon is finished, the satellite will perform a transfer to a graveyard orbit of GEO + 300 km.

MIS_050 GEO platform design reference ⌄

The satellite subsystems shall be designed and sized considering the new space assets that are being launched into GEO orbit in recent years and are planned to be launched in future years, and which use xenon for station-keeping. As a reference, the AIRBUS full-electric spacecraft Eurostar Neo should be considered.

MIS_060 Maximum wet mass ⌄

The total mass of the satellite at launch (wet mass), including the structure, subsystems, xenon payload and propellant for its own manoeuvres, shall not exceed 4,500 kg.

MIS_065 Launch dimensional envelope ⌄

The satellite’s dimensions shall not exceed the following values: 5.2 m for the diameter and 11.8 m for the height.

MIS_070 Xenon payload capacity ⌄

The satellite shall have a minimum payload capacity of 500 kg of xenon to supply different client satellites.

MIS_080 Xenon payload storage ⌄

The satellite’s tank shall ensure the safe storage of the xenon payload throughout the mission’s operational lifetime, maintaining its structural integrity and preventing leaks under the conditions of the space environment.

MIS_091 Autonomous rendezvous and proximity operations ⌄

The spacecraft shall be capable of autonomously executing rendezvous and proximity operations with a cooperative client spacecraft during predefined servicing windows and within the operational GEO region.

MIS_092 RPO communication availability ⌄

During critical rendezvous and proximity-operation phases, telemetry and command capability with the AstroFUEL ground segment and coordination data exchange with the client operator shall be available under nominal conditions.

MIS_093 Communication-loss collision safety ⌄

Loss of ground or client communication shall not result in loss of collision safety; the spacecraft shall autonomously transition to a predefined safe RPO state.

MIS_100 Approach corridor and keep-out zones ⌄

The spacecraft shall be capable of approaching a cooperative client spacecraft from the initial relative-navigation acquisition point to the docking interface while remaining inside the predefined approach corridor and outside all defined keep-out zones.

MIS_101 500 m hold point and GO/NO-GO criteria ⌄

A nominal hold point shall be established at a relative distance of approximately 500 m from the client, at which relative navigation shall be validated before continuing the approach.

Continuation from each hold point shall only be permitted when predefined limits on relative position, relative velocity, attitude error, navigation uncertainty and available abort capability are satisfied.

The corresponding numerical limits shall be defined by the RPO GNC and docking requirements.

MIS_110 Autonomous safety response ⌄

The spacecraft shall autonomously detect unsafe conditions during rendezvous, proximity operations and docking and shall initiate the corresponding safety response without requiring ground intervention.

Abort or retreat shall be triggered when at least one predefined safety limit is exceeded, including:

  • loss or unacceptable degradation of the relative-navigation solution;
  • violation of the approach corridor or keep-out-zone constraints;
  • relative closing velocity above the permitted limit;
  • loss of sufficient translational or attitude-control authority;
  • critical propulsion or GNC failure;
  • unexpected client motion or attitude;
  • violation of the minimum collision-avoidance capability.

The safe state following an abort shall correspond to a collision-free relative trajectory with increasing or non-decreasing separation from the client and sufficient distance to allow mission reassessment before any further approach attempt.

MIS_120 Xenon transfer system ⌄

The satellite shall have a system capable of transferring xenon to the client satellite under vacuum and microgravity conditions, using the necessary connection, pressure regulation and flow control mechanisms to ensure a safe and controlled transfer, while respecting the operating pressure and temperature limits of both spacecraft.

MIS_130 Continuous ground communication ⌄

The satellite shall maintain continuous communication with the ground, enabling operators to monitor all the mission phases and to command an abort in case of safety-critical concerns.
