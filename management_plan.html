Introduction

LAZARUS is focused on demonstrating an in-orbit refuelling service for satellites in geostationary orbit. The mission requires the coordination of several technical areas working together on the spacecraft, the refuelling payload, mission operations and launcher-related activities.

For this reason, a clear management structure is required to define responsibilities, organize the work, establish the main project phases and plan the activities needed throughout the development. This Management Plan provides the common framework used by the different technical areas to coordinate their work and follow the evolution of the project.

The document presents the internal organization of astroFUEL for the LAZARUS mission, the Project Breakdown Structure (PBS), the project phasing, the project planning, the main project risks and the Work Breakdown Structure (WBS). Both the preliminary design stage and the complete project lifecycle are considered, providing a general view of how the mission is expected to progress from its initial definition to operations and End of Life.

2

Project Organization

2.1 Identification of the customer

Market Definition: 2030s-2040s GEO Landscape

Propellant exhaustion is the principal limitation in the design life of a satellite and the primary barrier to unlocking immense financial value in geostationary orbit. An estimated one satellite out of roughly five in Geostationary Orbit (GEO) is shut down just because it ran out of station keeping fuel, even though its electronics, transponders, and solar panels still work perfectly.

LAZARUS overcomes this limitation. Its main market includes high capacity commercial telecom satellites and secure government defense assets operating in GEO using Xenon based Electric Propulsion (EP). Major manufacturers such as Airbus and Thales Alenia Space are moving toward standard servicing ports on their satellites before launch.

TOTAL ADDRESSABLE MARKET

GEO fleet

The entire global fleet of commercial and defense satellites operating in GEO during the roughly 2030s and estimated 2040s.

SERVICEABLE AVAILABLE MARKET

Xenon EP + servicing ports

The growing segment of next generation GEO satellites equipped with Xenon EP systems and standardized servicing ports.

SERVICEABLE OBTAINABLE MARKET

Initial campaign

The initial roughly 5 to an estimated 10 high capacity satellites managed by European commercial and defense operators.

Customer Profiles and Fleet Alignment

AstroFUEL targets operators who manage valuable space assets weighing between roughly 1,000 kg and an estimated 6,500 kg at launch.

Commercial Fleet Operators

Tier 1 companies such as Eutelsat Group, SES, Hispasat, Intelsat and Viasat. Their main goal is to keep fully paid-off satellites generating lease revenue for longer.

Government and Defense Organizations

Institutions such as Hisdesat, the French Directorate General of Armaments and allied defense ministries. Maintaining secure payloads online and preserving orbital control are primary objectives.

ROUGHLY 3,400 TO >6,500 KG Heavy and High Capacity Platforms

Airbus Eurostar Neo and Thales Alenia Space Spacebus Neo, including programs such as Spainsat NG I and II and Skynet 6A.

APPROXIMATELY 4,500 KG Software Defined Architectures

Mid-to-heavy platforms such as the Thales Alenia Space Space Inspire line.

ROUGHLY 1,000 KG SmallGEO Platforms

Newer and smaller satellite designs using electric propulsion, such as SWISSto12's HummingSat.

Operational Sizing and Contracting Strategy

LAZARUS carries a payload of approximately 500 kg of Xenon gas. Heavy assets such as Eurostar Neo and Spacebus Neo consume roughly 15 to an estimated 21 kg of Xenon per year, mid-class platforms such as Space Inspire use approximately 11 to around 15 kg, and SmallGEO platforms such as HummingSat use roughly 4 to an estimated 5 kg.

According to the economic figures used in the Management Plan, extending active service can represent a potential commercial gain approaching 50 million dollars for roughly six months of operation, or upwards of 100 million dollars for an estimated additional year.

SERVICE 01

Deep Life Extension

Roughly 100 kg of Xenon can be delivered to an estimated five heavy satellites, adding approximately five to roughly seven years of revenue-generating life to each asset.

SERVICE 02

Broad Fleet Coverage

Roughly 50 kg can be distributed across an estimated ten mid-class or defense satellites, adding an estimated three to roughly four years of operational service.

AstroFUEL works as a long-term planning partner. Customers sign Service Level Agreements approximately two to roughly three years before they run out of fuel.

The Early Adopter Strategy

LAZARUS initially focuses on European leaders such as Eutelsat Group, SES and Hisdesat. The first clients are expected to be forward thinking operators implementing standard refuelling ports in their satellites.

2.2 Company Organization

AstroFUEL is a private initiative in the European new space market. The company manages the complete spacecraft development process in-house, from mission analysis and subsystem design through manufacturing, integration, and final assembly.

External suppliers are used exclusively for the procurement of the materials, components, and parts required for the spacecraft. The integration and assembly of these elements into the spacecraft are carried out entirely in-house by astroFUEL.

To enable fast and effective decision-making, the company follows a clear chain of command. The CEO is the head of the company and oversees all ongoing projects, allocating the available company resources to each project according to its needs. The CEO appoints a Project Manager for each project.

Since astroFUEL's first project, LAZARUS, is for now only a technology demonstrator at a preliminary design stage, the company has only technical areas, pending the move to operate as a commercial enterprise.

PBS

3.1 PBS of the Preliminary Design Project

The Preliminary Design PBS outlines the physical and document deliverables required during the initial study and design phases (Phases 0, A, and B).

The structure focuses on defining the core mission architecture: the Spacecraft Bus, the Refueling Payload, preliminary Operations Deliverables, and early Launch Interfaces. The Ground Segment is excluded from this preliminary tree because its detailed development, validation, and procurement begin later in Phase C.

3.2 PBS of the complete Project

The Complete Project expands upon the preliminary foundation to represent the entire LAZARUS mission lifecycle, extending through End of Life disposal (Phase F).

It integrates the Ground Segment Facilities, including Ground Stations and Mission Control Software, and provides a comprehensive, lower-level breakdown of all major segments.

Risk Management

6.1 Identification of main risks

The LAZARUS mission focuses on developing an orbital refuelling infrastructure to extend the life of satellites in geostationary orbit. Its most critical segment is the refuelling maneuver itself.

Due to the demanding nature of the mission, the project carries a series of risks that must be carefully evaluated, managed and limited.

1. Not accurate docking maneuver
LAZARUS must perform delicate rendezvous and docking maneuvers to correctly approach the client satellite. High accuracy is required to prevent collision, interference with the client mission and the creation of new space debris. The docking mechanism must also ensure safe separation once the transfer is complete.
2. Malfunctioning during the refueling
During the transfer phase, the fuel pump, pressurization system and standard refuelling interface must work correctly. A malfunction or leak could alter the required Xenon conservation conditions and render the refuelling operation useless.
3. Fuel waste and Delta-V management
LAZARUS must complete repeated orbital maneuvers to service multiple clients. Unexpected debris avoidance maneuvers or higher-than-expected Delta-V requirements may reduce propellant reserves and prevent the spacecraft from servicing later clients or reaching the graveyard orbit.
4. Component performance and lack of flight heritage
Newly developed components may not perform in the space environment exactly as they did during tests on Earth. Innovative mechanisms such as the docking system are particularly important because failure could prevent interaction with the client.
5. Schedule Risks
Significant development or launch delays may prevent LAZARUS from reaching and refuelling the client in time, leading to a breach of contract and severe economic repercussions.
6. Market and economics risks
The mission is exposed to Xenon price volatility and limited global supply, as well as the possible loss of a pre-contracted client satellite before LAZARUS can service it.

6.2 Characterization of main risks in terms of impact and probability

Once the primary risks are identified, they are characterized by evaluating their likelihood of occurrence and the severity of their consequences on the mission’s performance requirements.

1. Not accurate docking maneuver
Autonomous proximity operations are affected by sensor noise, lighting variations and target dynamics. The mitigation strategy includes redundant proximity sensors, extensive development testing and autonomous collision-avoidance software capable of immediately aborting the maneuver if predetermined tolerances are exceeded.
2. Malfunctioning during the refueling
High-pressure Xenon transfer stresses valves, pumps and seals over repeated refuelling cycles. Mitigation requires severe ground testing, including TVAC testing under representative vacuum and thermal cycles, together with redundancy in isolation valves.
3. Fuel waste and Delta-V management
Debris avoidance and rendezvous operations can require more Delta-V than expected. This risk is mitigated through proactive trajectory analysis and a calculated propellant margin dedicated to unexpected maneuvers.
4. Component performance and lack of flight heritage
The mitigation strategy includes a rigorous technology maturation plan, early environmental testing, the study of lessons learned from previous fluid-transfer experiments and early involvement of manufacturing teams during prototyping.
5. Schedule Risks
Schedule risk is managed through appropriate schedule margins according to client timing requirements and through parallel development pathways for the most critical payload components.
6. Market and economics risks
Xenon commodity risk is mitigated through long-term fixed-price supply contracts. The client-loss risk is mitigated by maintaining a prioritized list of secondary GEO clients and versatile trajectory optimization models.

7

WBS

7.1 WBS of the Preliminary Design Project

The Preliminary Design WBS defines the work required to develop LAZARUS up to the Preliminary Design Review, covering the activities associated with Phases 0, A and B.

Its objective is to provide a structured decomposition of the work to be performed during the preliminary design stage, ensuring consistency with the project planning, responsibility assignment and schedule defined in Section 5.1.

The WBS is organized around the Space Segment (WP-1000) and Launcher (WP-3000). The Space Segment is further divided into Subsystems (WP-1100), Payloads (WP-1200) and Operations (WP-1300).

Activities belonging to later development phases are outside the scope of this preliminary WBS and are included in the Complete Project WBS presented in Section 7.2.

WP Scope Phases

7.2 WBS of the complete Project

The LAZARUS WBS covers the entire mission lifecycle (Phases 0+A to F), establishing the baseline for planning, cost control, and risk management.

Structure and Numbering. The WBS uses a four-digit hierarchical code: thousands for Level 1, hundreds for Level 2, tens for Level 3 and units for Level 4.

Level 1 consists of Management (WP-0000), Space Segment (WP-1000), Ground Segment (WP-2000), and Launcher (WP-3000).

The project scope extends through EOL disposal (Phase F, WP-1350). Subsystem equipment, whether developed in-house or procured, is integrated into its respective subsystem work package. Launcher selection & interfaces, campaign, and operations are consolidated within the Launcher segment (WP-3000).
