01 / OUR FIRST MISSION

Lazarus

A second life for spacecraft.

Satellite operations often end when onboard propellant is depleted. Lazarus is astroFUEL's first conceptual mission: an orbital servicing spacecraft designed to deliver xenon to client satellites and support the extension of their operational lifetimes.

The spacecraft uses xenon for both its electric propulsion system and the refueling service. These functions use two dedicated xenon tanks: one for Lazarus's own propulsion and one for the xenon payload transferred to clients. Its standby orbit is 50 km above nominal GEO.

AN ACADEMIC CONCEPT UNDER DEVELOPMENT · NOT AN OPERATIONAL SERVICE

04 / MISSION PROFILE

From Earth to orbital refueling.

MISSION PHASE 01

Launch & Orbital Insertion

Following launch and injection into GTO, Lazarus uses its xenon electric propulsion system to progressively increase orbital energy, reduce the orbit eccentricity and reach its GEO + 50 km standby orbit. The transfer is represented as a continuous low-thrust orbital evolution driven by electric propulsion.

02
MISSION PHASE 02

Service Request & Rendezvous

Between services, Lazarus remains in its GEO + 50 km standby orbit. When a client requests refuelling, the mission is planned for that target and the spacecraft begins the phasing and rendezvous sequence required to reach the client satellite in GEO. The orbital phasing and approach are performed progressively with xenon electric propulsion. As the relative distance decreases, navigation and guidance transition toward close-proximity operations for the final docking sequence.

03
MISSION PHASE 03

Final Approach & Docking

After rendezvous, Lazarus transitions to close-proximity operations. Relative-navigation sensors and the AOCS/GNC system guide the final approach, align the spacecraft with the client interface and support a controlled docking. If an unsafe condition is detected, the approach can be aborted to restore a safe separation between both vehicles.

04
MISSION PHASE 04

Docking & Xenon Transfer

Once docking is established, the connection is checked and the refuelling operation begins. Xenon is transferred from Lazarus' dedicated payload tank to the client spacecraft through the fluid-transfer system while the operation is monitored and controlled. The xenon used for Lazarus' own electric propulsion remains stored in a separate propulsion tank.

05
MISSION PHASE 05

Undocking & Return to Standby

After the required xenon has been transferred, the fluid-transfer path is isolated, the vehicles demate and Lazarus performs a controlled separation. Electric propulsion is then used to return the servicing spacecraft to its GEO + 50 km standby orbit, where it waits for the next client request.

REPEATED SERVICING CYCLE

Mission Phases 02 to 05 are repeated for successive client satellites throughout Lazarus' operational service life. Phase 06 is executed once, at the end of the mission.

06
MISSION PHASE 06

End-of-Life Disposal

At the end of the servicing mission, Lazarus leaves the active GEO region and is transferred to its disposal orbit above GEO. The final sequence includes the required disposal manoeuvre, spacecraft passivation and the End of Mission declaration.

One vision. More than one mission.

astroFUEL is the umbrella identity for a proposed space-systems venture. Lazarus is our first mission concept, focused on orbital refueling.

Our approach

Explore space infrastructure concepts that may enable longer-lasting, more flexible missions. Further concepts may be developed under the astroFUEL identity.
