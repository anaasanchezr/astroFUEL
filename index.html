01 / OUR FIRST MISSION

LÁZARO

A second life for spacecraft.

Satellite operations often end when onboard propellant is depleted. Lázaro is astroFUEL's first conceptual mission: an orbital servicing spacecraft designed to deliver xenon to client satellites and support the extension of their operational lifetimes.

The proposed system combines xenon storage, rendezvous, docking and in-orbit transfer. Its reference operating orbit is 50 km above nominal GEO.

AN ACADEMIC CONCEPT UNDER DEVELOPMENT · NOT AN OPERATIONAL SERVICE

02
MISSION PHASE 02

Service Request & Rendezvous

Once a client satellite requests assistance, Lázaro leaves its standby orbit at GEO + 50 km and performs the required orbital maneuvers to reach the client's geostationary orbital slot.

The spacecraft coordinates its rendezvous with the client while remaining within the operational and safety constraints imposed by the assigned GEO slot.

03
MISSION PHASE 03

Final Approach & Docking

After reaching the client satellite's vicinity, Lázaro begins the final proximity operations required for docking.

The propulsion and AOCS subsystems control the relative position, velocity and attitude of the spacecraft, allowing both vehicles to achieve the alignment and stability required for a safe docking maneuver.

04
MISSION PHASE 04

Docking & Xenon Transfer

Once docking is completed, the universal refueling interface establishes the mechanical and fluid connection between Lázaro and the client spacecraft.

Xenon is then transferred under controlled pressure, temperature and flow conditions, providing the client satellite with the propellant required to extend its operational lifetime.

WHY XENON?

Designed for long-duration missions.

Xenon is widely used as a propellant in electric propulsion systems, making it especially relevant for satellites designed to remain operational for many years.

Its physical properties allow a significant quantity of propellant to be stored compactly at high pressure, while its chemical inertness simplifies long-term storage and handling aboard the spacecraft.

01
High atomic mass

Heavy xenon ions provide useful momentum transfer when accelerated by electric propulsion systems.

02
Compact storage

High-pressure storage allows the spacecraft to carry a substantial xenon payload within a limited tank volume.

03
Chemically inert

As a noble gas, xenon does not chemically react with spacecraft components during long-term storage.

04
Long-life missions

Electric propulsion is particularly suited to spacecraft requiring efficient propellant use over extended operational lifetimes.

05
MISSION PHASE 05

Undocking & Return to Standby

After completing the xenon transfer, Lázaro performs a controlled undocking and establishes a safe separation from the serviced spacecraft.

The vehicle then executes the required orbital maneuvers to return to its standby orbit at GEO + 50 km, where it remains ready to receive the next refueling request.

06
MISSION PHASE 06

End-of-Life Disposal

At the end of its operational lifetime, Lázaro retains the propellant required to perform its final disposal maneuver.

The spacecraft is transferred away from the operational geostationary region and placed into a graveyard orbit, reducing interference with active GEO satellites.

REUSABLE SERVICING CYCLE

Phases 02–05 are repeated throughout Lázaro's operational lifetime, allowing the spacecraft to service multiple client satellites before performing its final disposal maneuver.
